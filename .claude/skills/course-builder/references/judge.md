# Judging a draft against the document

The validator proves the shape. The judge proves the content is the document's. Read the PDF and the
draft side by side and score each dimension 0–1; the overall score is the minimum of the four, not
the mean — a course with perfect coverage and invented codes is not 0.75, it is unpublishable.

| Dimension | 1.0 means | 0.5 means | 0 means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Every section of the document that a student is examined on is a topic, and every content statement under it is a key idea or inside one | Sections missing, or ideas that lump several statements together | Whole components or options absent |
| **Fidelity** | Every code, weight, mark total, minute count and option rule matches the document | A few numbers rounded or codes reformatted | Codes or weights that do not appear in the document |
| **Options** | Options match the document's choices exactly, and `topic.option` is set on each choice | An option listed but a choice missing | A non-optional section marked optional, or the reverse |
| **Family fit** | Marking style, command words and case-study/worked-example/practical content follow the family rules for this subject | Rules partly followed (e.g. a science with no practicals listed) | The wrong family |

Also report:

- `invented`: codes or topics in the draft that you cannot find in the document. Any at all caps
  fidelity at 0.4.
- `missing`: document sections with no topic. Each one caps coverage at 0.7.
- `changes`: anything the document says about changes from a previous issue, errata, withdrawn
  options, or first-assessment dates — the monthly pass files these into its proposal.
- `notes`: one paragraph a teacher would find useful.

**Publish at 0.8 or above.** Below that the draft goes to the review queue with this report attached,
so the admin sees what to fix rather than a bare refusal.
