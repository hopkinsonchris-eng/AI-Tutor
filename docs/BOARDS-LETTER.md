# Draft: asking a board for permission to mark against its papers

Send from your own address to the copyright contact: AQA copyright@aqa.org.uk · Pearson copyrightPQS@pearson.com ·
OCR via the contact form under "Copyright" at ocr.org.uk. Keep the paragraph on what is and is not stored exactly as
it is; it describes the platform truthfully. Until a board replies, its switch on the Admin tab decides whether
its papers can be marked; opening them on the board's own site is unaffected either way.

---

Dear [copyright team],

I run Study Platform (studyplatform.co.uk), a small revision site used by a handful of students in my family and
their friends, studying [qualification names and codes].

The site helps a student sit one of your published past papers and then mark their own handwritten answers.
I am writing to ask for your permission for the way it uses your papers and mark schemes, and to describe it
exactly.

What the site does with your material:

- It lists the papers and mark schemes you have published on your own website, and opens them there, on your
  domain. It does not copy, host or cache any of them.
- When a student asks for their answers to be marked, the site passes the web address of the paper and of the
  mark scheme to an AI model along with photographs of the student's own handwriting, and the model marks the
  answers. The paper and mark scheme are read for that purpose only; no copy is written to the site's storage.
- What the site keeps is: the student's photographs; a list of the questions on the paper with their marks and
  the specification topic each tests (numbers only, no question text); the marking points rewritten in different
  words, so a student can tick what their answer met; and the marks and feedback for that student.
- Nothing of yours is shown to the student inside the site other than the link to your own copy.

The site is not for sale and carries no advertising. Only the papers you have already released publicly are
listed; the ones you keep for teachers are shown as locked until your published release date.

I would be grateful if you could confirm that this use is acceptable, or tell me what you would need changed.
If you would prefer the site not to read your papers in this way, I will switch that feature off for your
papers; the links to your own site would remain.

Yours faithfully,

[your name]
[your address and telephone]
